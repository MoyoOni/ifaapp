# Healthy boundaries with those who guide us

A good Babalawo, Ìyánifá, elder or teacher is a gift. The relationship rests on
trust, and trust is kept safe by boundaries that protect both of you. This page
is here so that everyone in our community knows what care looks like, and what
to do if something does not feel right.

## What care looks like

- **You are always free to ask questions.** A guide who cares for you will
  explain what they are asking of you and why, in words you can follow.
- **You are always free to say no, or to take time.** You can decline, pause,
  or seek a second opinion from another practitioner without being punished,
  shamed or frightened.
- **Costs are clear before you agree.** What a reading, an offering or a
  guidance plan costs is stated up front. On Ìlú Àṣẹ, payments go through the
  platform and are held until the work is done.
- **Your family, friendships and other relationships are respected.** Spiritual
  guidance does not require cutting yourself off from the people in your life.
- **Your body is your own.** Physical or sexual contact is never a condition of
  guidance, and no one may ask for it in the name of the tradition.
- **Your private life stays private.** What you share is held in confidence,
  and you are not pressured to share more than you want to.

## Signs that something is wrong

Please reach out if a guide:

- frightens you into decisions, for example by saying terrible things will
  happen to you or your family unless you pay, obey or keep a secret;
- asks for more and more money, gifts or work beyond what you agreed, or
  pressures you to pay outside the platform;
- tells you to keep your relationship with them secret from family, other
  elders or the community;
- tries to control who you see, where you live, your money or your
  relationships;
- makes any sexual approach, or touches you in a way you did not welcome;
- becomes angry or threatening when you ask a question or say no.

None of these are part of genuine care. Raising a concern is not disrespect.

## What you can do

- **Talk to someone you trust** — a family member, another elder, or a member
  of your temple community.
- **Raise a concern with us.** On a practitioner's profile you can file a
  concern (for example "Controlling behavior", "Fear-based manipulation" or
  "Financial exploitation"). It goes to our elders and admins, who review it
  with care and in confidence.
- **Ask for a wellbeing check-in** from Community Care, or bring a conflict to
  the **Healing & Reconciliation** space for elder-led mediation.
- **If you are in danger**, contact your local emergency services first. You
  can also find support organisations on the Community Care page, or write to
  us at hello@iluase.com.

Whatever happens, you will be listened to. Seeking help is a strength, and our
community is stronger when everyone in it is safe.
