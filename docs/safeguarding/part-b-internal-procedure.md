# Ìlú Àṣẹ — Internal Safeguarding and Case-Handling Procedure

**INTERNAL — NOT FOR PUBLICATION.** Part B of the Safeguarding and Community Protection Policy.

Version 1.0 — draft for review, 3 November 2026 · Owner: Ìlú Àṣẹ Platform Owner · Reviewers: Platform Owner and appointed Cultural/Elder Advisory Representatives

This procedure turns the member-facing commitments in Part A into who does what. Where it names a platform tool, that tool exists today unless Part C marks it otherwise; where Part C marks a capability as missing, follow the manual workaround given here until it is built.

---

## 11. Governance and accountability

Safeguarding must have named owners. It must never depend only on informal conversations, goodwill, or the discretion of one elder.

### 11.1 Platform Owner
Accountable for: a working safeguarding framework; reporting routes that are watched; appointing the people below; decisions being recorded; being able to restrict accounts; escalating serious risks; and reviewing these arrangements. **Commercial interest, a practitioner's popularity or the platform's reputation must never outweigh a member's safety.**

### 11.2 Safeguarding Lead
A named person, with safeguarding experience or training, who:
- receives and records every report and assesses the risk (§13);
- decides the response pathway and co-ordinates investigations (§15);
- keeps confidential case records and monitors interim measures (§14);
- communicates with the people involved; and
- recommends outcomes (§16).

Where possible this is **not** the person who recruits practitioners or manages commercial relationships. Until a Safeguarding Lead is appointed (Part C, decision 1), the Platform Owner holds this role.

### 11.3 Cultural and Elder Advisory Representatives
Appointed elders may explain cultural context, advise on respectful communication, suggest community support, and advise whether a proposed response respects the traditions involved. However:
- cultural authority never replaces the safeguarding assessment;
- no elder may dismiss a report as "disrespectful";
- elders declare conflicts of interest (§17) and never handle a case about themselves or someone they are close to;
- advisers see only the case information they need, never the full case record; and
- a report about an elder is handled independently of that elder.

### 11.4 Administrators, moderators and Community Care carers
They must recognise and escalate safeguarding concerns, preserve evidence, and protect confidential information. They must **not** investigate serious allegations themselves or promise outcomes; they refer to the Safeguarding Lead. Everyone in these roles must know who to contact when a report concerns the Safeguarding Lead or the Platform Owner (§17).

## 12. Receiving reports and keeping records

**Where reports arrive today:**
- the **Practitioner Complaints** tab in the admin dashboard — concerns filed from a practitioner's profile (`PractitionerComplaint`); urgent-looking ones, matched against the admin's keyword rules, are sorted first and notify every admin;
- the **Crisis Alerts** tab — crisis language detected in forum posts, circle posts and comments, and remembrance wall entries (claim → follow up → record the outcome);
- **wellbeing check-in requests**, handled by Community Care carers; and
- email to [safeguarding@iluase.com / hello@iluase.com].

Each report gets a case reference (today the complaint's id; for email reports, create a complaint record or log entry the same day). The case record holds: reference; date received; category; who or what role was reported; a brief description; initial risk assessment; immediate measures; assigned handler; status; decisions and reasons; communications and follow-up; and closure date.

Case records are seen only by authorised people. Never discuss a case in community spaces, general moderation channels or unrestricted admin notes. *Gap: today every admin can see every complaint (Part C, SAFE-03).*

**Anonymous reports** must still be assessed. The lack of a named reporter may limit what can be investigated or fed back, but is never a reason to dismiss a report. *Gap: no anonymous route yet (SAFE-10); an email from an unidentifying address counts.*

**Acknowledgement:** the target is within two working days, and urgent safety concerns are assessed as soon as they are seen. These are internal targets; they are not advertised to members until staffing can meet them (Part C, decision 12).

## 13. Risk assessment and priority

Assess the **risk**, never the status or reputation of the person reported. Ask:

1. Is anyone in immediate physical danger?
2. Is there a risk of sexual exploitation or violence?
3. Is it still happening?
4. Does the reported person have authority over the person affected?
5. Are there threats, intimidation or coercion?
6. Is there financial exploitation?
7. Could other members be affected?
8. Could evidence be lost?
9. Is the reporter at risk of retaliation?
10. Is outside help needed?

| Priority | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Critical** | Threat to life or safety; sexual assault or credible threat of violence; coercion presenting immediate danger | Immediate safety assessment; emergency escalation (§20); consider suspending the reported account at once (§14); preserve evidence; assign a decision-maker |
| **2 — High** | Ongoing financial exploitation; repeated intimidation; controlling behaviour; sexual harassment; retaliation against a reporter | Prompt assessment; consider temporary restrictions; open a formal investigation; watch for risk to other members |
| **3 — Standard** | Boundary concerns; inappropriate messages; misrepresented services; non-urgent confidentiality concerns | Review the evidence; ask for clarification; decide on corrective action or formal investigation |

A priority describes urgency. It is not a judgement about whether the allegation is true.

## 14. Interim protective measures

While a concern is assessed or investigated, the Safeguarding Lead may, proportionately:

- **suspend the account** (admin → Users → Suspend, or "Suspend bookings" when resolving a complaint) — this removes the person's ability to act on the platform for the period set;
- hide or restrict community participation via moderation tools; and
- pause money that is still held: a disputed escrow is frozen and is not auto-released (the 14-day auto-release skips disputed escrows and orders with an open return).

*Gaps (SAFE-05): there is no narrower "stop new bookings / hide profile only" restriction and no "block contact between these two people"; account suspension is the available measure. Direct messaging is paused platform-wide, so private platform contact is not currently possible anyway.*

Interim measures are precautions, not findings. Record each one with its reason, review it regularly, and tell the restricted person of the restriction and the general reason unless that would create a safety risk or compromise evidence.

## 15. Investigation

**Stage 1 — Initial review.** Identify the conduct alleged, the people and accounts involved, available evidence, immediate risks, conflicts of interest (§17), and whether external referral is needed.

**Stage 2 — Preserve evidence.** Platform records (bookings, payments, escrow and order history, forum and circle content, complaint evidence links), screenshots the parties provide, and earlier related reports. Keep originals where practicable and record who accessed what. *Note: soft-deleted content stays in the database; do not hard-delete anything connected to an open case.*

**Stage 3 — Gather information** from the reporter, witnesses and the person reported. The reporter is never required to meet or communicate with the reported person. The reported person gets a fair opportunity to respond to the substance, subject to safety and evidence considerations.

**Stage 4 — Assess** the consistency of accounts, supporting evidence and records, the responses given, relevant context, and whether the platform's standards were breached. **Cultural standing, religious experience, popularity or contribution to the platform never decide the outcome.**

**Stage 5 — Findings**, recorded against each allegation: substantiated; partly substantiated; not substantiated; inconclusive; or unable to determine. "Not substantiated" never means the report was dishonest; lack of evidence is never treated as proof of fabrication.

**Stage 6 — Outcome.** Record the decision, reasons and actions (in the complaint's resolution notes). Update the reporter appropriately — resolving a complaint notifies them and points them to the Healing & Reconciliation space. Tell the reported person the outcome and any restrictions on them, subject to privacy and safety.

## 16. Outcomes

Where a breach is established, one or more of: guidance or corrective instruction; written warning (admin → Warn); required safeguarding or conduct training; restrictions on communication or on specific services; temporary suspension; removal of practitioner status; permanent removal (admin → Ban); and referral to external authorities.

Weigh: seriousness; repetition; harm or risk caused; whether exploitation was deliberate; co-operation with the process; and risk to other members. Upheld complaints also lower the practitioner's trust score automatically.

Serious sexual misconduct, violence, coercion or deliberate exploitation may justify immediate suspension and permanent removal, after an appropriate assessment and a fair process. **Money brought in, reputation and personal relationships are never reasons to overlook serious misconduct.**

## 17. Conflicts of interest and independence

Anyone handling a case declares family, financial, close personal, teacher–student or organisational ties, and anything else that could affect impartiality. Anyone with a material conflict does not decide the case or control the investigation.

A complaint about the Platform Owner or the Safeguarding Lead goes to [named independent person or external safeguarding professional — Part C, decision 2]. If independence cannot be secured internally, seek external advice or investigation.

## 18. Mediation and reconciliation safeguards

Healing & Reconciliation supports appropriate dialogue. It is not a substitute for safeguarding, and must never be used to avoid investigating serious misconduct, pressure someone to withdraw a report, protect a prominent practitioner, extract forgiveness, silence allegations of abuse, or prevent external reporting.

Participation is voluntary. Cases involving sexual misconduct, violence, serious threats, coercion or significant exploitation do not go to ordinary community mediation. Any future restorative process in such cases needs specialist safeguarding advice, a safety assessment and genuinely voluntary participation, and never replaces accountability.

## 19. Confidentiality and data protection

Limit access to authorised people; collect only what the case needs; store records securely; never discuss cases in community spaces; avoid unnecessary disclosure to elders, practitioners or members; use secure communication; and follow a retention schedule (Part C, decision 11).

Confidentiality is not absolute: information may be shared to protect someone from serious harm, to meet a legal obligation, or to refer to an authority. Where safe, tell the person concerned before a significant disclosure. Never promise complete anonymity or absolute confidentiality.

## 20. External referrals and emergencies

Ìlú Àṣẹ recognises the limits of its authority. Where a concern suggests a crime, immediate danger or serious risk, the Safeguarding Lead considers referral to: emergency services; police; specialist sexual-violence support; adult safeguarding services; child protection (if a minor may be involved, although membership is 18+); and relevant regulatory or professional bodies. The platform never replaces a criminal investigation or specialist support. Each referral is judged against the law, immediate safety and the person's circumstances. Keep the organisations in the admin **Support directory** current; it is what members are shown.

## 21. Appeals

A person directly affected by a formal disciplinary decision may ask for a review because relevant evidence was overlooked, there was a material procedural error, a decision-maker had an undisclosed conflict, new material evidence has emerged, or the sanction was disproportionate. Wherever practicable, the review is done by someone not involved in the original decision. A review does not automatically lift protective restrictions. *Gap: no appeal workflow in the platform yet (SAFE-14); handle by email and record the outcome on the case.*

## 22. Records and reporting to the Platform Owner

The Safeguarding Lead keeps the confidential case record. The Platform Owner receives a periodic, anonymised report: concerns received by category; response times; investigations opened and closed; outcomes and sanctions; repeat concerns about the same practitioner; outstanding risks; and training or policy changes. Serious incidents are escalated straight away, not saved for the report.

## 23. Training

Before taking on responsibilities, explain these duties to practitioners (at onboarding), elders and cultural representatives, administrators and moderators, and Community Care carers. Cover: recognising coercion and exploitation; boundaries; consent; confidentiality; receiving disclosures sensitively; preserving evidence; escalating; avoiding retaliation; and conflicts of interest. Practitioners accept the safeguarding standards as part of joining (SAFE-12).

## 24. Review

Review this policy at least yearly; after any serious incident; after material platform changes; when relevant law changes; and when elders, practitioners or members identify an improvement. The Platform Owner keeps the current approved Part A accessible to members.
