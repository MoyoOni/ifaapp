# Safeguarding and Community Protection

**Your rights, what we expect of everyone, and how to get help**

Version 1.0 — draft for review, 3 November 2026

---

## 1. Our commitment

Ìlú Àṣẹ exists so that people can learn about and take part in Yoruba spiritual tradition — with practitioners, elders and one another — with dignity, respect and confidence.

People often come to a Babalawo, Ìyánifá or elder at a time of uncertainty, grief or an important decision. That trust is precious. It must never become a way to frighten, exploit or control anyone.

Cultural respect and personal safety belong together. We are committed to a community where:

- everyone is treated with dignity;
- practitioners are accountable for how they behave;
- you can ask questions and make your own decisions;
- spiritual guidance is never used to justify pressure or exploitation;
- your boundaries, privacy and consent are respected;
- you can raise a concern without fear of retaliation; and
- every concern is taken seriously and handled fairly.

This page sets the minimum standards for everyone taking part in Ìlú Àṣẹ. It does **not** define Ifá doctrine, prescribe religious practice, or decide the authority of any lineage. When a concern is raised, we look at what was done and its effect on the person — not at whether a spiritual claim was correct.

## 2. Who this covers

Everyone using or representing Ìlú Àṣẹ: members, Babalawos, Ìyánifás and other practitioners, elders, teachers and mentors, vendors, moderators, administrators and Community Care carers.

It covers what happens on the platform, and conduct elsewhere that arises from a relationship begun through Ìlú Àṣẹ. Ìlú Àṣẹ is for adults aged 18 and over.

## 3. Your rights

### You may ask questions
You can ask a practitioner what they are recommending, why, and what it involves, and you should get an answer you can understand. You do not have to accept an explanation just because it comes from an elder or an experienced practitioner.

### You may say no, or take time
You can decline a reading, recommendation, offering, ceremony or any other request. You can pause, stop working with someone, or seek another practitioner's view. No one may threaten, shame, frighten or punish you for doing so.

### You should know the cost first
You should know what a service costs before you agree to it. Any additional service or charge must be explained and agreed separately. No one may use fear, urgency or spiritual threats to get more money from you.

When you pay through Ìlú Àṣẹ, your payment is held, not passed straight to the practitioner or vendor. It is released when you confirm the work was done or your order arrived — or automatically 14 days after they mark it delivered, unless you raise a problem first. No one should press you to pay outside the platform, where this protection does not apply.

### Your relationships are your own
You should never be pressed to cut yourself off from family, friends or others you trust. A practitioner may offer advice about relationships, but must not use spiritual authority to control your social life.

### Your body is your own
No practitioner, elder, teacher or anyone representing Ìlú Àṣẹ may ask for sexual contact, intimate images or unwanted physical contact as a condition of guidance, healing, teaching or taking part. Consent must be freely given and can be withdrawn at any time. Someone's title, age, experience or spiritual standing never removes their duty to respect your boundaries.

### Your private life stays private
You should not be pressed to share personal, financial, medical, family or intimate information that the service you chose does not need. What you share must be handled with care and seen only by people with a real reason to see it.

### You may raise a concern
You can raise a concern about anyone covered by this policy. You do not need proof before reporting something that makes you feel unsafe. A concern raised in good faith is never disrespect towards Yoruba culture, Ifá, an elder or this community.

## 4. What we expect of everyone

Everyone taking part in Ìlú Àṣẹ is expected to:

1. treat others with dignity, fairness and respect;
2. respect personal boundaries and consent;
3. be honest about services, costs and expectations;
4. never intimidate, manipulate or threaten;
5. respect each person's right to make their own decisions;
6. keep confidential information confidential;
7. never take advantage of someone's vulnerability or reliance on them;
8. co-operate with safeguarding enquiries;
9. report serious concerns where someone's safety may be at risk; and
10. respect any restrictions applied under this policy.

Practitioners must keep appropriate professional and personal boundaries, including in private conversations with members.

## 5. Recognising harm

These are examples, not a complete list.

**Fear-based manipulation** — saying you or your family will suffer unless you pay or obey; demanding instant compliance through fear; bringing new threats to get more money or obedience.

**Financial exploitation** — undisclosed or ever-rising charges; pressing you to borrow money for spiritual services; asking you to pay outside the platform to avoid its protection; taking money or gifts by deception or pressure.

**Controlling behaviour** — demanding you end relationships; controlling where you live, work or spend money; demanding secrecy about the relationship; monitoring your communications; threatening you for seeking another opinion or leaving.

**Sexual misconduct and unwanted contact** — sexual advances made from a position of spiritual authority; demanding sexual contact for guidance or services; unwanted touching; pressing for intimate images; using spiritual explanations to override consent.

**Harassment, intimidation and retaliation** — threatening someone who asks questions; humiliating or exposing someone who raised a concern; threatening to exclude, shame or discredit a person for reporting; harassing someone after they end an engagement.

## 6. How to raise a concern

If something makes you uncomfortable, please tell us — even if you are unsure how to describe it. You can report your own experience, something you saw, or behaviour you believe puts someone else at risk.

**About a Babalawo or Ìyánifá:** use **Raise a concern** on their profile and choose the category closest to what happened: [controlling behaviour, fear-based manipulation, financial exploitation, sexual misconduct or unwanted contact, harassment or intimidation, breach of confidentiality, fraud, or another concern]. You can follow the status of your concern from your account.

**About anyone else** (an elder, vendor, member, moderator or someone representing Ìlú Àṣẹ), **or if you would rather not use the form:** write to [safeguarding@iluase.com] — or [hello@iluase.com] — or ask for a **wellbeing check-in** from Community Care.

Share what you are comfortable sharing. You do not need to give more personal detail than is needed to understand the concern.

### What happens next
A designated safeguarding lead looks at every concern. Depending on what it involves, they may:

- contact you to understand what happened;
- check whether anyone is in immediate danger;
- review platform records, such as bookings and payments;
- put temporary protective measures in place;
- open a formal investigation; and
- where necessary, involve emergency services or other appropriate authorities.

We will keep you informed as far as privacy, safety and a fair process allow. **You will never be asked to confront the person you reported.**

## 7. If you are in danger

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, **contact your local emergency services first.** Do not wait for us.

If you have experienced sexual assault, violence, threats or another crime, you can also contact local support services or the police. Organisations that can help are listed on the **Community Care** page.

Ìlú Àṣẹ can support you on the platform, but it is not an emergency service, the police, or a replacement for professional help.

## 8. Healing, reconciliation and its limits

Community dialogue and reconciliation matter to us, and the **Healing & Reconciliation** space offers elder-led mediation for conflicts.

Mediation is not right for every situation. Concerns involving sexual misconduct, violence, coercion, serious intimidation, exploitation or a large imbalance of power are always handled through the safeguarding process first.

No one will be pressed to meet, confront, forgive or reconcile with someone they have reported. Choosing not to take part in mediation never affects the support you receive.

## 9. Protection from retaliation

We do not tolerate retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith or helps with an enquiry — including threats, harassment, unwanted contact, public shaming, damaging someone's reputation, pressure to withdraw a report, exclusion from the community, or further financial or spiritual threats. Retaliation is itself a breach of this policy.

## 10. Our responsibility

We will make every reasonable effort to provide accessible ways to report; to look at concerns promptly and handle them sensitively; to take proportionate steps to reduce risk; to protect your personal information; to treat everyone involved fairly; to hold practitioners and everyone representing Ìlú Àṣẹ accountable; and to keep improving how we do this.

We cannot promise that every concern will be upheld, or that every outcome will satisfy everyone. We do promise to take every concern seriously, follow a fair process, and act when the evidence and circumstances call for it.

> No one's spiritual standing, knowledge, title or influence places them beyond accountability. And no one should ever feel that asking a question, setting a boundary or seeking protection is an act of disrespect.
